## Day 1 — Monday
**Topic:** Greetings and politeness
**Time:** ~1 hour

### Review (10 min)
Nothing to review yet. Set up instead:
- Install **Anki** and create a deck called *Français*.
- Start a notebook with 3 sections: *Leçons*, *Mes erreurs*, *Journal*.

### Lesson (20 min)
French has two "you" words. Choosing the right one is your first cultural skill.
- **tu** is for friends, family, children and people your age in casual settings.
- **vous** is for strangers, older people, work, shops, and *always* when in doubt. It's also the plural "you".

| French | English | When |
|---|---|---|
| Bonjour | Hello / Good morning | Until ~6 pm, to anyone |
| Bonsoir | Good evening | After ~6 pm |
| Salut | Hi / Bye | Friends only (tu) |
| Au revoir | Goodbye | Anyone |
| À bientôt / À demain | See you soon / See you tomorrow | Anyone |
| Merci (beaucoup) | Thank you (very much) | Anyone |
| De rien | You're welcome | Anyone |
| S'il vous plaît / S'il te plaît | Please (vous / tu) | Match tu/vous |
| Excusez-moi / Pardon | Excuse me / Sorry | Getting attention / bumping into someone |
| Oui / Non | Yes / No | |
| Madame / Monsieur | Madam / Sir | *Bonjour, Madame.* It's polite to add these. |

**Culture tip:** In France you *always* say *Bonjour* when entering a shop, a bus or a lift. Skipping it is rude.

## Day 2 — Tuesday
**Topic:** The alphabet and accents
**Time:** ~1 hour

### Review (10 min)
Anki. Then say out loud: how do you greet a shopkeeper at 8 pm? (*Bonsoir, Monsieur/Madame.*)

### Lesson (20 min)
**The alphabet.** Watch Alexa's alphabet video, then read along:

A *a* · B *bé* · C *sé* · D *dé* · E *euh* · F *ef* · G *jé* · H *ash* · I *i* · J *ji* · K *ka* · L *el* · M *em* · N *en* · O *o* · P *pé* · Q *ku* · R *er* · S *es* · T *té* · U *u* (lips round, say "ee") · V *vé* · W *double vé* · X *iks* · Y *i grec* · Z *zed*

The traps: **E** (*euh*) and **I** (*i* like "ee"); **G** (*jé*) and **J** (*ji*); **U** has no English equivalent.

**Accents** change the sound or the meaning:
| Accent | Example | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| é (accent aigu) | *café, été* | Sound "ay" |
| è (accent grave) | *mère, très* | Sound "eh" |
| à, ù (grave) | *à* (to), *où* (where) | Only distinguishes meaning: *a/à*, *ou/où* |
| ê (circonflexe) | *fête, forêt* | Sound "eh"; often a lost *s* (forest → forêt) |
| ç (cédille) | *ça, français* | Makes c sound like s before a, o, u |
| ë (tréma) | *Noël* | Pronounce the vowel separately |

Useful phrase: **Comment ça s'écrit ?** (How is that spelled?)

### Practice (15 min)
Spell these out loud, letter by letter, then check yourself with the alphabet:
1. Your first name  2. Your surname  3. LAGOS  4. ABUJA  5. JEUDI (a trap: J then E)
Write in your notebook: *Je m'appelle ___. Ça s'écrit ___.*

## Day 3 — Wednesday
**Topic:** Je m'appelle… + être + numbers 0–10
**Time:** ~1 hour

### Review (10 min)
Anki. Spell your name out loud without looking.

### Lesson (20 min)
**Saying your name**
- *Comment tu t'appelles ?* (informal) / *Comment vous appelez-vous ?* (formal): What's your name?
- *Je m'appelle Chidi.*: My name is Chidi.
- *Enchanté* (said by a man) / *Enchantée* (said by a woman): Nice to meet you.

**Être (to be), singular forms**
| | |
|---|---|
| je suis | I am |
| tu es | you are (informal) |
| il est / elle est | he is / she is |
| vous êtes | you are (formal) |

*Je suis nigérian(e). Tu es étudiant ? Elle est ingénieure.*

**Numbers 0–10:** zéro, un, deux, trois, quatre, cinq, six, sept, huit, neuf, dix
Silent letters: *deux* sounds like "deuh"; the *t* in *huit* is heard alone but *huit* often links: *huit_heures*.

**Check:** 1. suis 2. es 3. est 4. êtes 5. est

## Day 4 — Thursday
**Topic:** The sounds of French
**Time:** ~1 hour

### Review (10 min)
Anki. Count 0–10 out loud, then backwards.

### Lesson (20 min)
Five rules that explain most of French pronunciation:
1. **Final consonants are usually silent**: *petit, grand, vous, Paris*. Except for **C, R, F, L** (think *CaReFuL*): *avec, bonjour, neuf, avril*. (And *-er* at the end of verbs is silent: *parler* = "parlay".)
2. **Final -e is silent**: *madame, France, table*.
3. **Nasal vowels**: the vowel goes through your nose and the *n/m* disappears: *an/en* (*France, enfant*), *on* (*bonjour, non*), *in/ain/un* (*vin, pain, un*).
4. **u vs ou**: *tu* (lips round, tongue high, "ee" sound) vs *tout* ("oo"). *Dessus* ≠ *dessous*!
5. **Liaison**: a silent final consonant is pronounced when the next word starts with a vowel: *vous_êtes* ("voo-zet"), *les_amis* ("lay-zamee"), *deux_enfants* ("deuh-zanfan").

The French **r** is made at the back of the throat, like a gentle gargle. Practise *rouge, Paris, merci*.

### Practice (15 min)
Cross out the silent letters, then read each word aloud:
1. *salut*  2. *avec*  3. *grand*  4. *bonjour*  5. *petite*  6. *parler*  7. *neuf*  8. *vous*

**Check:** Silent: salu~~t~~, gran~~d~~, petit~~e~~ (the t *is* heard because the e follows it; only the e is silent), parl~~er~~ (sounds "parlay"), vou~~s~~. Pronounced: avec, bonjour, neuf.

## Day 5 — Friday
**Topic:** Ça va ? Small talk
**Time:** ~1 hour

### Review (10 min)
Anki. Read yesterday's five rules out loud.

### Lesson (20 min)
| Question | Answers |
|---|---|
| *Ça va ?* (informal) | *Ça va bien, merci. / Très bien ! / Pas mal. / Bof… / Pas très bien.* |
| *Comment allez-vous ?* (formal) | *Je vais bien, merci. Et vous ?* |
| *Et toi ?* / *Et vous ?* | And you? |

**French phone numbers** are said in pairs: 06 12 34 56 78 → *zéro six, douze, trente-quatre, cinquante-six, soixante-dix-huit*. (You'll learn the big numbers soon. For now, read the digits one by one.)

A full mini-dialogue:
> — Bonjour Madame, comment allez-vous ?
> — Très bien, merci. Et vous ?
> — Je vais bien, merci. Je m'appelle Tunde.
> — Enchantée, Tunde. Moi, je suis Madame Leroy.
> — Au revoir, Madame. Bonne journée !
> — Au revoir !

*Bonne journée* (have a good day) / *Bonne soirée* (have a good evening) are said when leaving.

### Practice (15 min)
Write two dialogues of 6 lines each:
1. Informal: you meet a friend at church.
2. Formal: you meet your new manager.

Then read both aloud.
